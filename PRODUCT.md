# ANM Accounting

## Register
brand

## Product Purpose
A marketing website for ANM Accounting, an independent chartered accounting firm based in Glasgow, Scotland. The site exists to make a serious, confident first impression on prospective clients evaluating accountants for their business — and to convert qualified enquiries into booked discovery calls.

It is not a client portal, not a SaaS product. It is the digital storefront and credibility document for the firm.

## Users
**Primary: Scottish SME owners (founders, directors, sole traders).**
- Glasgow and the wider central belt — construction, hospitality, professional services, e-commerce, creative agencies, healthcare practices.
- Annual turnover £100k–£10M. Between 1 and 50 employees.
- Currently either (a) self-managing books in QuickBooks/Xero and outgrowing it, (b) unhappy with their current accountant (slow, reactive, only does compliance), or (c) starting a business and needing a partner from day one.
- They care about: trust, responsiveness, proactive tax advice, fixed transparent fees, someone who actually picks up the phone. They are tired of being "another file" at a bigger firm.

**Secondary: HNW individuals needing personal tax & self-assessment.**
- Property landlords, contractors, company directors with complex affairs.

**Tertiary: Referral partners (solicitors, IFAs, bank relationship managers).**
- They visit briefly to vet the firm before recommending it onward.

## Brand & Tone
- **Confident, not corporate.** ANM is independent and proud of it. The voice should sound like a senior partner talking to a peer at a coffee — direct, knowledgeable, occasionally dry. Never salesy. Never "synergy" language.
- **Glasgow, not London.** Plain-spoken. Practical. No City-of-London polish. A Scottish reader should feel this firm understands them; an English reader should feel it's serious.
- **Editorial gravitas.** The site should feel closer to a Financial Times feature or Monocle profile than a SaaS landing page. Long-form copy is welcome where it earns its place.
- **Numbers as identity.** Tax bands, deadlines, fee structures, savings figures — treat numbers as first-class design elements, not afterthoughts.

## Anti-References
What this site must NOT look like:
- **Big 4 corporate stock** — handshakes, glass towers, gradient blue-to-purple, "Welcome to [Firm Name]" hero with generic tagline.
- **Fintech mint-teal** — Mercury, Wise, Ramp aesthetic. ANM is not a tech product.
- **Local-business WordPress template** — three-column "Our Services" with icons, a contact form below, beige.
- **The hero-metric template** — "£X million saved for clients" big number, supporting stats, gradient accent. SaaS cliché.
- **Glassmorphism, gradient text, side-stripe accent borders, identical card grids.** Bans.
- **Excessive AI/automation language.** The differentiator here is human expertise, not technology.

## Strategic Principles
1. **Trust is earned through specificity.** Real numbers, real services with prices where possible, real names and faces, real Glasgow address. Vague claims read as fake.
2. **The first call is the conversion event.** Every page funnels toward "book a free 30-min discovery call." That CTA is always one click away but never shouty.
3. **Show, don't claim.** Don't say "we are responsive." Show a stated response-time guarantee. Don't say "proactive tax planning." Show a worked example of what that looks like for a typical client.
4. **Calm pacing.** The reader is a busy business owner. Big breathing room, scannable hierarchy, but room for depth where they want to slow down.
5. **One CTA per page section.** Never compete with yourself.
